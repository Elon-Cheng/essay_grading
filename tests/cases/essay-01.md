## Student planning notes

### Structure

- Paragraph 1: greeting and recommendation to choose Course B.
- Paragraph 2: Course B uses a teaching method that moves from easy to difficult; this is suitable for newcomers, helps students absorb and digest knowledge, and builds confidence. Course A may cause anxiety because its method is unclear.
- Paragraph 3: Course B uses assessment methods involving class performance and homework, which provide external motivation and improve efficiency. The multi-aspect assessment is friendly to slow but earnest learners. Course A relies heavily on self-discipline.
- Paragraph 4: conclusion, wishes and ending.

### Vocabulary notes

- absorption / digestion
- persevere in
- on the contrary / however
- outside force / self-discipline
- multi-aspect / one-sided
- a striking effect
- ensure efficiency
- concerning / careful / earnest

## Essay

Dear Kevin,

I heard from you that you intend to take part in a programming class, and there're two different types of courses for you to choose from. Whether you have decided or not, I'm writing to you to suggest that you should choose course B. The reasons follow.

First of all, the B courses' teaching method which transits from easy to difficult is more reasonable especially for newcomers like you. We must follow the principle of gradual improvement while learning anything, because that leads us to better absorption and digestion of new knowledge. Meanwhile, as you have a clear understanding about the difficulty of future study, you can preview the lessons and keep in perfect order. In that case, you can build up your confidence which helps you persevering in future study. On the contrary, the teaching style of course A, which is said to be "making concrete analysis", seems a bit mysterious because, its difficulty can suddenly add up if the teacher deepen the courses, but you know nothing about it. That sounds embarrassing and causes anxiety.

Moreover, the checking measures of course B is strict but increase efficiency. By bringing your class performance and homework into the terminal score, it's a type of outside force to require you to do everything carefully and perfectly. It sounds rigid and boring, but it really can bring out a more striking efficiency. However, course A does need self-discipline, for they may never check your homework. Furthermore, course B is also friendly for the slow but earnest learners like you, because testing score only occupy one-third of the final score, which means even if you can't digest that well before the test, you will also achieve a high score through your hard work. In comparison with it, course A's testing means seem really one-sided, and demands on-site performance rather than diligence, which is, in my view, a little bit unfair.

That's my reason of why to choose course B, but after all, the choice is up to you. Anyhow, I genuinely wish you enjoy your programming study and your efforts can yield good results.

Yours sincerely,
Li Hua
